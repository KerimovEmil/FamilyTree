Notes & Biography

Born May 22, 1903 in Amirjan (Əmircan), Baku (previously estimated as 1906). Studied law and served as a judge/jurist in Khizi (Xızı), where he married Kursum (Gülsüm) Kerimova, and later in Baku.
Drafted into the Red Army in 1941 (Crimean Front) during WWII; captured in 1943 and worked as an archivist/journalist for the "Azərbaycan" newspaper in Berlin (1943-1945).
Held in an Allied POW camp in Naples, Italy (1945); rescued from forced Soviet repatriation in February 1946 by King Farouk of Egypt, managing the royal agricultural estate near Cairo (1946-1948).
Emigrated to Turkey in 1948, taking the name Ali Haydar Aras ("Gemiçli Haydar Bey"), managing estates in Istanbul and Gemiç (Gemlik, Bursa), and marrying Nazik Belin in 1951.
Reconnected with his family in Baku by letter in 1966 with the help of conductor Maestro Niyazi shortly before passing away from a heart attack on March 7, 1967 in Gemiç, Gemlik.
His life story is documented in the 340-page biographical book "Yaman Ayrılık" ("Yaman ayrılıq", 2017) written by his adopted daughter Samiye Kaplan.
Courtship of Kursum in Khizi: Eager to see Kursum's face despite local modesty customs, Aligeidar arranged to drop a bucket of water from the second floor so that she would look up, allowing him to see her face and instantly fall in love. When her father Halig refused permission for the marriage, Aligeidar used his judicial authority to temporarily hold Halig in jail. Halig dispatched a relative to Amirjan to investigate Aligeidar's lineage; upon arriving in Amirjan, the very first person the emissary met and questioned turned out to be Aligeidar's own uncle.
